# options-pricing-project
Applied Deep Learning project comparing neural networks with Black–Scholes for SPY option pricing.

This project will be investigating the use of deep learning to predict the prices of financial option using historical market data. Options are financial derivatives that give the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset, such as a stock or exchange-traded fund (ETF), at a specified price under agreed conditions. Traditional pricing models, such as Black–Scholes, estimate option values by assuming that the underlying asset price follows geometric Brownian motion with constant volatility. However, their theoretical prices may differ from observed market prices. This project will investigate whether feed-forward neural networks trained on historical market data can estimate observed option prices more accurately than the Black–Scholes model.

The neural network will be trained using historical publicly available market data with input features including the underlying asset price (the current market price of the stock or ETF), the strike price (the specified price at which the asset can be bought or sold under the contract), time to expiration (the time remaining until the option expires), option type (a call option gives the holder the right to buy the asset, while a put option gives the holder the right to sell it), and historical volatility (a measure of how much the underlying asset's price has fluctuated in the past). The project will investigate how different neural depths, widths and activation functions will affect prediction accuracy. Optimization methods such as stochastic gradient descent with momentum and ADAM will also be compared.
The key outputs will include a trained neural network, a comparison of different model configurations, and visualisations of prediction errors. Performance will be evaluated using mean absolute error (MAE), which measures the average absolute difference between predicted and observed prices, and root mean squared error (RMSE), which penalises larger prediction errors more heavily. The final neural network and black scholes will be evaluated on the test set. Test-set pricing errors will be visualised to compare the neural network with Black–Scholes and examine how accuracy varies by option type, moneyness and time to expiration.
The project will use publicly available SPY ETF options quotes from 2020–2022. The midpoint of the bid and ask prices will serve as the target price. Data preparation will address missing values, invalid quotes and unusually wide bid–ask spreads. The inputs and assumptions required for the Black–Scholes benchmark will be documented to support interpretation of the comparison.
The data will be divided chronologically into training, validation and test sets, keeping all observations from each quote date together. Preprocessing will be fitted using training data only, and features derived directly from the target price will be excluded to reduce data leakage. The network will be trained to minimise mean squared error using backpropagation to calculate gradients. Validation performance will guide model selection, while the test set will remain unused until final evaluation. The analysis will acknowledge limitations, including the use of the European-style Black–Scholes model to benchmark SPY options, which allow early exercise.
